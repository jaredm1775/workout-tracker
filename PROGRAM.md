# PPL × Upper/Lower (5 days, to failure)

Jared's program. It's a 5-day hybrid in the style of Jacob Oestreicher's PPL × Upper/Lower: low volume, with every working set taken to failure. Each muscle gets trained twice a week.

Sessions take about 40–55 minutes. Log every working set in the app. The rest timer starts when you mark a set done.

This is general training guidance, not medical advice. Skip or substitute anything that hurts a joint.

## How hard: technical failure

1. **What failure means.** It's the last rep you can finish with full range of motion and the same form. If a rep slows to a grind and barely makes it, that was the last one. Stop once the next rep would need body English, a shortened range or a spotter.
2. **Warm up first, then go all-out.**
   - Before the first exercise for each muscle, do 2–3 ramp-up sets: about 50% × 8, 70% × 5 and 85% × 2–3.
   - Rest 30–60 s after the light ramp-ups. Rest 1:30–2:00 after the last one, before your top set.
   - For later exercises on a muscle that's already warm, one feeler set is enough (about 60% × 5).
   - Don't log warm-ups. The first set you log is the all-out top set.
3. **Set 2 is a back-off set.** Keep the same weight and expect 1–3 fewer reps. If that would drop you below the rep range, take off about 10%. Set 2 also goes to failure.
4. **Calibrate in weeks 1–2.** Stop when you think you have 1 rep left, then try it. If it goes up cleanly, you weren't at failure, so push further next time. Most people underestimate how far they are from failure by 2–4 reps.
5. **Safety.**
   - On barbell squats, bench, RDLs and overhead press, stop 1 rep short (RPE 9) unless you have a spotter or safeties set.
   - On machines, cables, and the Smith machine with its stoppers set, true failure is fine.
   - Never grind through joint pain.

## Progression (top-set rule)

Most lifts use 5–9 reps. Lateral raises, rear delts, face pulls and calves use 8–12.

When your **first** set reaches the top of the range with clean form, add the increment next session. Set 2 doesn't need to hit the top. The app's "Next" weight follows this rule.

Typical increments:
- **+5 lb** on presses, rows and pulldowns
- **+10 lb** on leg press, hack squat and hinges
- **+2.5 lb** on small cable and dumbbell work

## No Smith machine?

Tap **Swap** on the exercise and pick something you can safely take to failure:
- **Presses:** a machine press, or dumbbells you can drop at failure.
- **Squats:** a hack squat, pendulum, belt squat or leg press.
- **Barbell in a rack with the safety pins set:** this works too, but stop 1 rep short.

## Deload

After each 8-week block, or whenever your joints feel beat up, take a deload week: 1 set per exercise, stopping about 2 reps short of failure (2 RIR). Then go back to the normal sets.

## How to use the app

1. Pick today's day, or the suggested "Next" day.
2. Enter the weight and reps, then tap **Done** on each set.
3. The rest timer starts on its own. Use −15 / +15 or Skip. Your actual rest time is saved on the next set.
4. **Swap** (on exercises that have alternatives) changes the lift for today's session. Each alternative keeps its own history and "Next" weight. Swapping locks once you've logged a set for that exercise.
5. **+ Note** opens a small note on the exercise, for things like seat height or a one-off machine. Next session it shows up as "Last note". Notes appear in History and sync to the Sheet.
6. Tap **Finish** when the session is done.
7. Use Calendar to see which days you trained this week and this month.
8. Export JSON from Backup if you want a copy. That file is private unless you put it in the public repo.
